Construction Schedule and Cost Risk Tool

A schedule and cost simulator for Indian building projects. It shows the range of likely finish dates, costs and returns, not just one number, and how much contingency a project really needs.

What our case study shows

A G+10 residential building with 28 activities, simulated thousands of times.

Why simulate?

A single schedule gives one finish date, and it is usually too optimistic. Real projects have uncertain durations, risks such as monsoon or approval delays, and activities that slip together. The tool runs the project thousands of times and reports how often each outcome happens, so contingency is set from evidence instead of a flat percentage.

What it does

Realistic schedule

Runs the project thousands of times, letting every activity take a realistic range of durations, and shows the chance of finishing by any date.

Delays that move together

Links activities that tend to slip at the same time, such as outdoor work in the monsoon or work done by the same crew.

Risk events

Adds events like approval delays or labour shortages, each with its own chance of happening. Switch any risk on or off to see what it costs.

Contingency and returns

Shows how much contingency you really need, and how delays change the project's NPV, IRR and loan interest.

Is a fix worth it? What does delay cost?

Compares the cost of reducing a risk with the value it protects, and shows what every day of delay costs the project.

Checked by hand

Every calculation was checked against hand calculations and an independent Excel model, and the results are documented.

How it works

  1. Enter each activity with its best-case, most likely and worst-case duration and cost, and what must finish before it starts.
  2. Add risk events and group activities that tend to slip together.
  3. Enter the project's finance details: land, sale price, loan terms.
  4. Run 10,000 simulations.
  5. Read the likely finish dates, the contingency needed compared with a flat 10%, which activities and risks drive delay, and the effect on returns.

Limitations and data privacy

Results are only as good as the inputs. The case study uses published rates and practitioner estimates and is an illustration, not advice for a real project. The schedule uses finish-to-start links only and 30-day months, and does not model taxes. The public app runs on Streamlit Community Cloud, so uploaded data passes through Streamlit's servers: for confidential project data, run the tool on your own computer from the code repository.

Use of AI

This tool was built with the help of AI assistants for research, coding and drafting. Every number was checked by the team against hand calculations and an independent spreadsheet, and all AI use is recorded in our AI usage log.
